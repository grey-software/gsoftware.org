Four apps, live today as alphas.

Zenshu Paper

A calculator gives you the answer. Paper gives you a quiet place to find your own.

Mental maths in ink and brush, a few calm minutes a day. Each trick is shown, then practised in short rounds. No stars, streaks or scores.

Open Paper at paper.zenshu.greysoftware.org

Zenshu Arcade

When a machine can do the working, there is still joy in learning the trick yourself.

The same tricks as a game, with stars, boss rounds and a daily streak. Freestyle mixes any kinds you pick, from times tables to fractions.

Open Arcade at arcade.zenshu.greysoftware.org

Zenshu Garden

Patience is part of the method. Every round takes the time it needs.

Times tables and mental maths in small, unhurried rounds. Each round you finish grows a shoot on your tree.

Open Garden at garden.zenshu.greysoftware.org

graphyti

A textbook is flat and a whiteboard is wiped by Monday. An idea in maths should be something you can turn over.

Lessons that take vectors, matrices, functions and surfaces one idea at a time. Drag an arrow and watch where a matrix sends it.

Open graphyti at graphyti.greysoftware.org

Others build things we believe in.

  • OSSU computer science

    A whole undergraduate path in computer science, built from free online courses, with a community to study alongside.

    Years of self-discipline, and no degree at the end.

  • Scrimba

    Interactive coding courses with lessons you can pause and edit to try the code yourself.

    Choose a course that matches what you want to build.

  • Boot.dev

    Backend development courses with coding exercises and guided projects.

    A structured path that takes regular practice.

  • roadmap.sh

    Community-built maps for learning a developer role or skill. The backend map connects languages, databases and APIs.

    Use the maps to plan what to learn, then build projects to practise it.

  • Project based learning

    Tutorials that teach a language by building a whole app in it.

    A long list of links, so quality varies.

  • Mapgen4

    A map generator by Red Blob Games that redraws as you paint, with how it works written up as a blog series.

    No longer maintained.

  • Apollo 11 guidance computer

    The 1969 flight software that landed on the Moon, kept as source to read.

    Written in the guidance computer's assembly. You read it, you do not run it.

  • Ladybird

    A new, independent browser engine, written from the web's own standards.

    Pre-alpha, and for developers for now.

  • News Feed Eradicator

    A browser extension that hides social media feeds so you can visit without scrolling through them.

    Hides feeds on supported sites, rather than blocking the whole site.
